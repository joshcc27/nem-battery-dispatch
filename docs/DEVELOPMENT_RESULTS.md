# Development reference results

These are the two **development** months: November 2022 and April 2023.
Every earlier design choice was made by looking at them, including the
4-hour pre-dispatch cutoff, the band spread and the error cap. They therefore
illustrate the model; they are not evidence for the study's conclusion. The
continuous validation and test results are in
[RESEARCH_NOTE.md](RESEARCH_NOTE.md), and the protocol that separates them is
[PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md).

Each month here is run on its own, with the hourly re-priced bands the
reference was built with. The final study instead fixes band prices for each
trading day, as the NEM requires, and carries SOC through twelve continuous
months. Its results therefore differ from these.

All layer figures come from `artifacts/analysis/layers.csv` and
`artifacts/analysis/layer_gaps.csv`, regenerated by

```powershell
uv run battery-dispatch verify-reference
uv run battery-dispatch reference
```

The asset is a hypothetical 100 MW / 200 MWh battery with 87% round-trip
efficiency at the Hornsdale 275 kV connection point in SA1, with a A$25/MWh
degradation cost. Every figure is wholesale-energy only.

Read [Limitations](#limitations) before quoting any number here.

All boundaries are 00:00 AEST. The warm-up month is read by the forecasters and
the scenario bootstrap but never dispatched or settled. All four months are
checked against their committed manifests before a result is computed.

## The layers

| Layer | Policy |
|---|---|
| `ceiling` | Whole month as one LP with the exclusivity binary relaxed. An upper bound under the stated model: it may charge and discharge at once and need not be attainable by any physical policy. |
| `oracle` | The layer-2 engine given the realised prices: an oracle benchmark. A finite rolling horizon does not make it the best policy at that cadence, although it comes within A$230 of the ceiling in both months. |
| `L2_persistence` | Forecast: the same interval yesterday. |
| `L2_profile` | Forecast: the median of the same interval over the last 7 days. |
| `L2_aemo` | Forecast: AEMO's own, as published by the decision time — P5MIN for the next hour, pre-dispatch up to 4 hours ahead, the 7-day profile beyond. |
| `L3_profile` | 10 equiprobable scenarios: the profile forecast plus the profile forecaster's realised error on a past day. |
| `L3_aemo` | The same around the AEMO forecast, from the AEMO forecaster's own past errors. |

Every policy except the ceiling re-solves each hour on a 24-hour look-ahead and
carries SOC forward. Layer 3 shares the committed hour across its scenarios and
optimises expected value; its scenario days are drawn from the last 21 with a
fixed seed, and every past error is capped at the pool's 5th and 95th
percentiles first.

### AEMO's forecasts, and why only four hours of pre-dispatch

The AEMO forecaster reads the MMSDM archive's every-run pre-dispatch and P5MIN
tables and uses, at each decision, the latest run *published* by then. Its
error by lead time, against the 7-day profile, sampled every six hours:

| MAE, A$/MWh | 0–1 h | 1–4 h | 4–12 h | 12–24 h |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| AEMO, Nov 2022 | 63 | 113 | 154 | 176 |
| Profile, Nov 2022 | 111 | 112 | 111 | 111 |
| AEMO, Apr 2023 | 27 | 33 | 85 | 162 |
| Profile, Apr 2023 | 64 | 59 | 60 | 62 |

Pre-dispatch is sharp for a few hours and worse than the naive profile a day
ahead, where it over-forecasts spikes. Used to its end it inflates the plan's
value of stored energy, so `L2_aemo` uses it up to four hours ahead and the
profile beyond. **That cutoff was read from these two months, so it is an
in-sample choice**; the sensitivity below shows what it is worth:

| Pre-dispatch used up to | `L2_aemo` Nov | `L3_aemo` Nov | `L2_aemo` Apr | `L3_aemo` Apr |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 hour (P5MIN only, in effect) | 44.7% | 42.3% | 42.9% | 44.8% |
| **4 hours** | **60.9%** | **55.5%** | **51.4%** | **54.3%** |
| its whole horizon | 58.0% | 52.5% | 48.5% | 52.8% |

### How the committed hour is executed

The next hour is not dispatched as fixed quantities. Each five-minute interval
is offered, as a NEM bid is, in ten equal price bands that clear against the
realised price. The bands come from `lam`, the plan's marginal value of one
more MWh stored at the end of the interval — the dual of its energy balance,
summed over scenarios in layer 3 — shifted by the quantiles of the
forecaster's own recent errors at the committed hour's lead times:

| | Band `k` clears when the realised price is | Band quantity |
|---|---|---|
| Charge | at or below `(eta_c * (lam + o_k) - tb) / L` | a tenth of the power rating |
| Discharge | at or above `((lam + o_k) / eta_d + deg + tb) / G` | a tenth of the power rating |

`o_k` is the error quantile at the band's midpoint, `tb` the A$0.0001/MWh
tie-breaker and `L`, `G` the dated loss factors. A band exactly at its limit
clears its share of the planned quantity. Quantities are capped by the SOC
headroom as the hour plays out, and where bands of both directions clear the
planned direction wins, then the plan's exclusivity mode, then the larger
margin.

A perfect forecast has no errors, so its bands collapse to the plan's own
limits — its optimality conditions — and it is executed exactly as planned:
`oracle` is A$3,608,856.15 under every execution. For a real
forecast the realised price can decline a planned trade or clear an
unplanned one, progressively as it moves past the bands, but it never enters
a decision; it only clears the offer. Bands spread by the committed hour's
errors are narrow where the forecaster is sharp: a median width of A$100–172
for AEMO, whose next hour is P5MIN, against A$202–391 for the naive
forecasters.

These are realised, out-of-sample differences on one month each. They are
not the expected values that stochastic programming calls the value of the

output columns are named accordingly: `realized_stochastic_uplift_*` and
`gap_ceiling_to_l3_*`.

Every decision in every layer solved to optimality with a MIP gap of 0, every
layer is below its ceiling, and every physical, exclusivity and settlement gate
passed. The whole run takes about 80 seconds on a laptop.

## Reading the layers

**The cadence is free; the forecast is not.** Hourly re-solving on a 24-hour
look-ahead with perfect prices reaches the bound to within A$230, and
reproduces the earlier 48-hour/daily rolling headline of A$3,608,856.15 to the
cent through a completely different engine. So the entire drop to layer 2 is
information.

**AEMO's forecasts are worth the most where prices are wildest.** In November
`L2_aemo` keeps 60.9% of the bound against 35.5% for the profile, A$916k
more. Almost exactly that difference is earned in the month's 11 intervals
above A$5,000: `L2_aemo` sells into every one of them for A$916k, where the
profile policy sells into three for A$311k. In April the
advantage is small, 51.4% against 49.3%: prices follow the daily shape a
7-day median already captures, and the one spike of the month fell to the
profile policy, not the AEMO one.

**Layer 3 helped in April and not in November, for reasons not yet
established.** In April, `L3_aemo` beats `L2_aemo` by A$51k and is the best
forecast layer of the month at 54.3%. In November it is A$193k worse. Around
the naive profile, layer 3 is A$81k worse in both months.

This document first explained November by AEMO's archive gap. With no October
pre-dispatch, the error pool is empty on 1 November, thinner than ten days for
the first 245 decisions, and full only from 22 November. That explanation was
a hypothesis, and a controlled comparison does not support it. April 2023 is
well covered, and was re-run with its error history deliberately thinned the
same way (`dev-2023-04-thin` in
[`results/development/`](../results/development/summary.md)). The thinning
did not lower revenue: averaged over three seeds, `L3_aemo` earned slightly
*more* with the thinned pool under both executions. The November shortfall
must come from something else, such as that month's spikes or the anticipative
scenario tails. The two months alone cannot separate these.

### Execution: bands, one limit, or committed quantities

`battery-dispatch reference --execution bid|committed` runs the same plans with a
single price limit per direction or with planned quantities dispatched at any
price. Share of the bound:

| Month | Layer | Ten bands | One limit | Committed quantities |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Nov 2022 | `L2_persistence` | 30.0% | 34.5% | −7.5% |
| Nov 2022 | `L2_profile` | 35.5% | 38.7% | −6.2% |
| Nov 2022 | `L2_aemo` | 60.9% | 60.4% | 28.1% |
| Nov 2022 | `L3_profile` | 33.3% | 35.2% | −11.7% |
| Nov 2022 | `L3_aemo` | 55.5% | 56.0% | 24.0% |
| Apr 2023 | `L2_persistence` | 40.3% | 41.8% | 41.9% |
| Apr 2023 | `L2_profile` | 49.3% | 45.4% | 54.9% |
| Apr 2023 | `L2_aemo` | 51.4% | 45.5% | 52.7% |
| Apr 2023 | `L3_profile` | 44.6% | 42.7% | 48.3% |
| Apr 2023 | `L3_aemo` | 54.3% | 53.5% | 54.5% |

- **Bidding is what makes the extreme month work.** Committed quantities lose
  money on the naive forecasts in November, almost all of it by charging at
  100 MW through price spikes of up to A$15,500 that the forecast put near
  −A$40. Any bid declines those.
- **Bands and one limit are level overall** — the ten forecast-layer shares
  sum to 455 and 454 — but they fail differently. A single limit sits at the
  plan's break-even price, typically a few dollars from the forecast, so in a
  calm month the realised price declines most planned trades: bands recover
  most of that, reaching 51.4% against 45.5% for `L2_aemo` in April. For the
  naive forecasters in November, whose errors are large even an hour ahead,
  wide bands clear only part of the power in the negative-price troughs a
  single limit charges through, and cost 2–5 points.
- **Committed quantities are best for every layer in a calm month** — up to
  54.9% in April — because following the plan's daily cycle regardless of the five-minute noise
  is what earns there. A bid is only as good as the value of stored energy it
  is priced at, and a forecast's is noisy.

Bands were first spread by errors pooled over the whole 24-hour look-ahead:
median widths of A$350–440 in November, and 2–7 points worse than a single
limit there. Spreading them by the committed hour's errors, which are the
prices they actually face, is the version reported.

### Capping past errors

Winsorizing past errors at the 5th and 95th percentiles helps layer 3 in
every case, if modestly. Share of the bound, capped against uncapped:
`L3_aemo` 55.5% against 52.1% in November and 54.3% against 54.0% in April;
`L3_profile` 33.3% against 33.0% and 44.6% against 42.9%. It does not close
the gap to layer 2 around the profile; the anticipative scenario tails are the
likelier remaining cause.

### Reading these scenarios

**Degradation cost drives both revenue and tractability.** It is the model's
only cycling penalty, so it sets how hard the battery works: 100.9 equivalent
full cycles at A$10/MWh against 54.6 at A$50/MWh. It also sets how many
exclusivity binaries survive the reduction — 1,988 at A$10/MWh against 170 at
A$50/MWh — which is why `degradation_10` takes ten times longer than every
other scenario. Extrapolating that trend is why A$0/MWh is out of scope; see
[VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md#solver-tolerance-and-where-exclusivity-binds).

**Endpoint effects are small here.** Starting at the 100 MWh midpoint adds only
A$549 over the headline, because the extra 90 MWh of unpriced opening inventory
is worth little against a month this volatile. Both cases end at the 10 MWh
floor.

**The salvage case now binds.** At A$400/MWh — above the realised spread — the
look-ahead salvage term holds 190 MWh at the end of the run instead of
depleting to the floor, and costs A$19,627 of cash revenue to do it. An earlier
A$100/MWh variant was dropped because it sat below the realised spread, never
changed the committed dispatch, and so tested nothing.
